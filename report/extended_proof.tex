\subsection{A hint-aware Fiat--Shamir reduction}\label{sec:fs-hints}

The security statement of~\cite[Theorem~1]{C:ABDPW25} is obtained by composing
three results:
\cite[Lemma~2.2]{C:ABDPW25}, which is~\cite[Lemma~3.5]{EC:AABN02}, reduces the
\EUFCMA security of $\SIG[\Sigma]$ to the \textsf{IMP-PA} security of $\Sigma$ at
the price of a multiplicative factor $\numRO+1$;
\cite[Lemma~3.1]{C:ABDPW25} then replaces the transcript oracle of the
\textsf{IMP-PA} game by the hint-assisted simulator, at the price of an additive
$\hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\cdot, \numOSign}$ term;
and \cite[Lemma~3.2]{C:ABDPW25} concludes by special soundness.
Because the second step is applied \emph{inside} the first one, the hint-assisted
\textsf{wHVZK} advantage ends up being multiplied by $\numRO+1$.

This is an artefact of the modularisation rather than of the reduction: the
transcript oracle of the \textsf{IMP-PA} game is only ever used to answer the
signing queries of the forger, and there are $\numOSign$ of those, not $\numRO$.
In this section we prove a single lemma that performs the two steps in the
opposite order, replacing the honest signing oracle by the hint-assisted
simulator \emph{directly inside the \EUFCMA game}, before the guessing argument
of~\cite{EC:AABN02} is invoked. In the resulting bound
$\hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\cdot, \numOSign}$ is a purely additive
term.

Throughout, $\Sigma = (\Prover_1, \Prover_2, \Verif)$ is a $\Sigma$-protocol for
a relation $R$ with challenge space $\cC$, the family $\hintdistr =
\set{\hintdistr_x}_x$ is a hint distribution for $R$
(\cite[Definition~3.1]{C:ABDPW25}), and $\Sim$ is a $\hintdistr$-hint-assisted
simulator for $\Sigma$ (\cite[Definition~3.2]{C:ABDPW25}). We write $\SIG[\Sigma]$
for the Fiat--Shamir signature scheme obtained from $\Sigma$, in which the
challenge of a signature on $\msg$ under the public key $\pk = x$ is computed as
$\chall \gets \RO(\com \concat x \concat \msg)$. Finally we write
\[
  \hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}
  \defeq
  \Pr\left[\hintdistr\text{-}\mathsf{hint}\text{-}\mathsf{IMP}\text{-}\mathsf{PA}_\Sigma(\bdv, \numOSign) = 1\right]
\]
for the advantage of a two-stage adversary $\bdv = (\bdv_1, \bdv_2)$ in the
$\hintdistr$-hint-\textsf{IMP-PA} game of~\cite[Definition~3.5 and
Game~3]{C:ABDPW25}: the algorithm $\bdv_1$ receives a statement $x$ generated by
$\Gen_R(1^\secp)$ together with $\numOSign$ hints $\hint_1 \until
\hint_\numOSign \gets \hintdistr_x$ and outputs a commitment and a state; it then
receives a uniform challenge $\chall \sample \cC$, and $\bdv_2$ must produce an
accepting response. Note that, unlike in the \textsf{IMP-PA} game, $\bdv$ has
\emph{no} access to a transcript oracle, and that this game is not in the random
oracle model: an adversary that needs a random oracle simulates it internally.

As is customary, we assume that the encoding $(\com, x, \msg) \mapsto \com
\concat x \concat \msg$ used by $\SIG[\Sigma]$ is injective and efficiently
invertible on its image; this is the case for \sqisign{}, where all three
components have a fixed length.

\begin{lemma}[Hint-aware Fiat--Shamir]\label{lem:fs-hints}
    Let $(R, \hintdistr)$ be a relation with hints, let $\Sigma$ be a
    $\Sigma$-protocol for $R$ with challenge space $\cC$, and let $\Sim$ be a
    $\hintdistr$-hint-assisted simulator for $\Sigma$.
    For any PPT algorithm $\adv$ against the \EUFCMA security of $\SIG[\Sigma]$
    making at most $\numRO$ queries to $\RO$ and at most $\numOSign$ queries to
    $\OSign$, there exist a two-stage PPT algorithm $\bdv = (\bdv_1, \bdv_2)$ and
    a PPT algorithm $\ddv$ such that
    \begin{align*}
        \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv)
        &\leq (\numRO + 1)\cdot \hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}
        + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign} \\
        &\phantom{\leq ~}
        + (\numRO + \numOSign + 1)\,\numOSign \cdot \minentropy{\Sigma}.
    \end{align*}
    The running time of $\ddv$ is that of $\adv$ plus one evaluation of
    $\Ver_\Sigma$ and $O(\numRO + \numOSign)$ operations on the random-oracle
    table; the running time of $\bdv$ is the same plus $\numOSign$ evaluations of
    $\Sim$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    We use code-based game playing~\cite{EC:BelRog06}, following the proof
    of~\cite[Lemma~3.5]{EC:AABN02}; the games are given in
    \Cref{fig:games1,fig:games2} and are the following.
    \begin{games}
        \game{gm0} The \EUFCMA game of $\SIG[\Sigma]$, with a lazily sampled
        random oracle.
        \game{gm1} The signing oracle overwrites the random-oracle table with a
        freshly sampled challenge instead of re-using a value already stored
        there. The flag $\badprog$ records when this happens.
        \game{gm2} The $\numOSign$ honest transcripts are sampled eagerly, so that
        $\mathbf{Initialize}$ runs the experiment $\Real_\Sigma(1^\secp,
        \numOSign)$ of~\cite[Experiment~2]{C:ABDPW25}.
        \game{gm3} The honest transcripts are replaced by hint-assisted
        simulated ones: $\mathbf{Initialize}$ now runs $\HintSim_\Sigma$ rather
        than $\Real_\Sigma$. This is the hop that reduces to hint-assisted
        \textsf{wHVZK}, and from here on the witness is unused.
        \game{gm4} A guess $\fpguess$ of the index of the random-oracle query
        underlying the forgery is drawn, and a second flag $\badev$ is set if the
        guess is wrong. The guess does not influence the output.
        \game{gm5} When the guess is wrong, the verifier is run on the challenge
        sitting at the guessed position instead of the correct one.
        \game{gm6} The guess and the challenge are drawn in
        $\mathbf{Initialize}$, and the challenge is planted at the
        $\fpguess$-th fresh random-oracle query. This game is the simulation
        carried out by the impersonator $\bdv$ in the
        $\hintdistr$-hint-\textsf{IMP-PA} game.
    \end{games}
    Both flags are initialised to \bfalse. They live in disjoint parts of the
    chain: $\badprog$ is only used in the hop $\printgame{gm0} \to
    \printgame{gm1}$ and is dropped from $\printgame{gm2}$ on, whereas $\badev$
    exists only in $\printgame{gm4}$--$\printgame{gm6}$. For a game $\bG$ we
    write $\bG^\adv \Rightarrow 1$ for the event that the game outputs $1$ when
    run with $\adv$, and for $4 \leq i \leq 6$ we let $\goodev_i$ be the event
    that the $i$-th game never sets $\badev$. Recall that two games are
    \emph{identical-until-$\badev$} if their code differs only in statements that
    follow the setting of the flag to \btrue, and recall the two standard
    consequences of this~\cite{EC:BelRog06}: for identical-until-$\badev$ games
    $\bG, \bG'$ and any adversary $\adv$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:fundamental}
        \abs{\Pr[\bG^\adv \Rightarrow 1] - \Pr[\bG'^\adv \Rightarrow 1]}
        \leq \Pr[\bG^\adv \text{ sets the flag}],
    \end{equation}
    where the right-hand side may equally be taken to be
    $\Pr[\bG'^\adv \text{ sets the flag}]$, since identical-until-$\badev$ games
    set the flag with the same probability; and, denoting by $\goodev, \goodev'$
    the events that the flag is never set,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:good}
        \Pr[\bG^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev]
        = \Pr[\bG'^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev'].
    \end{equation}

    \subsubsection*{Normalising the forger.}
    Exactly as in the proof of~\cite[Lemma~3.5]{EC:AABN02}, we may replace $\adv$
    by a forger $\adv'$ of essentially the same running time, making at most one
    extra $\RO$ query, with
    $\Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv) \leq
    \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv')$ and with the following three
    properties, where $x$ denotes the public key that $\adv'$ receives:
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item every $\RO$ query of $\adv'$ parses as $\com \concat x \concat
            \msg$, with middle component equal to $x$;
        \item before outputting a forgery $(\msg^*, \sig^* = (\com^*, \chall^*,
            \rsp^*))$, the algorithm $\adv'$ has queried $\RO$ on $\com^* \concat
            x \concat \msg^*$;
        \item $\msg^*$ was never queried to $\OSign$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Indeed, $\adv'$ runs $\adv$ and forwards its queries, except that a query
    which does not parse as in (i) --- either because it is malformed or because
    its middle component differs from $x$ --- is answered from a separate table,
    sampled lazily and uniformly at random by $\adv'$ itself. Such points are
    never programmed by $\OSign$ and never enter the winning condition, so this
    does not change the view of $\adv$; using a table rather than fresh
    randomness keeps the answers consistent on repeated queries. Property (ii) is
    obtained by making the query $\RO(\com^* \concat x \concat \msg^*)$ just
    before halting, and property (iii) does not decrease the advantage, since a
    forgery on a signed message never wins the \EUFCMA game. The forger $\adv'$
    makes at most $\numRO + 1$ queries to $\RO$, all of them conforming; from now
    on we write $\adv$ for $\adv'$, and $\numRO$ still denotes the query bound of
    the original forger.

    \subsubsection*{From the \EUFCMA game to the simulated game.}
    Game $\printgame{gm0}$ of Figure~\ref{fig:games} is the \EUFCMA game for
    $\SIG[\Sigma]$, written so that the random oracle is lazily sampled into a
    table $\HT$ and so that the list $\QT$ records, in order, the arguments of
    the \emph{fresh} $\RO$ queries of $\adv$; queries answered from $\HT$ and
    reprogrammings performed by $\OSign$ are not recorded. In $\printgame{gm0}$
    the signing oracle executes lines $23$--$26$, which set $\chall_\sctr
    \coloneqq \HT[u]$ if that entry is defined and $\chall_\sctr \sample \cC$
    otherwise; line $27$ then rewrites the same value and is a no-op in the first
    case. This is exactly the signing algorithm of $\SIG[\Sigma]$. Line $35$
    reads $\HT[u^*]$ directly rather than calling $\RO$, which is harmless
    because property (ii) guarantees that this entry is defined. Hence
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g0}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm0}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        = \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv).
    \end{equation}

    Game $\printgame{gm1}$ is obtained from $\printgame{gm0}$ by deleting the
    single line $26$: a signing query that hits an already defined table entry no
    longer re-uses the stored value, but overwrites it with the freshly sampled
    challenge $\chall_\sctr$. Line $26$ follows the setting of $\badprog$ at line
    $25$, so the two games are identical-until-$\badprog$, and
    \eqref{eq:fundamental} gives
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g0g1}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm0}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        \leq \Pr[\printgame{gm1}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        + \Pr[\printgame{gm1}^\adv \text{ sets } \badprog].
    \end{equation}
    We bound the second term. The commitment produced by $\Prover_1(x,w)$ is
    independent of the message being signed and of the history of the game, and
    by~\cite[Definition~2.4]{C:ABDPW25} it takes any fixed value with probability
    at most $\minentropy{\Sigma}$. When the $i$-th signing query is made, at most
    $(\numRO + 1) + (i-1)$ entries of $\HT$ are defined: at most $\numRO+1$ from
    the $\RO$ queries of $\adv$ and $i-1$ from the previous signing queries.
    Since the encoding $(\com, x, \msg) \mapsto \com \concat x \concat \msg$ is
    injective, the event $\HT[u] \neq \bot$ forces $\com_\sctr$ to equal the
    commitment component of one of those entries, whence
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:bad}
        \begin{aligned}
            \Pr[\printgame{gm1}^\adv \text{ sets } \badprog]
            &\leq \sum_{i=1}^{\numOSign} \bigl(\numRO + i\bigr)\minentropy{\Sigma}
            = \left(\numRO + \frac{\numOSign+1}{2}\right)\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma} \\
            &\leq (\numRO + \numOSign + 1)\,\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma}.
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    Game $\printgame{gm2}$ differs from $\printgame{gm1}$ only in that the
    $\numOSign$ transcripts consumed by the signing oracle are sampled
    \emph{eagerly}, in $\mathbf{Initialize}$, instead of on demand, and that
    $\badprog$ is dropped. This is possible precisely because in
    $\printgame{gm1}$ the value stored in $\HT$ no longer influences the
    transcript --- lines $24$--$25$ only touch the flag, which is itself dropped
    in $\printgame{gm2}$ --- so the triple
    $(\com_i, \chall_i, \rsp_i)$ is a function of $(x,w)$ and of fresh randomness
    only, and in particular it depends neither on the message $\msg$, nor on the
    queries of $\adv$, nor on the random oracle. Hence $\printgame{gm2}$ is a
    perfect rewriting of $\printgame{gm1}$ (transcripts that are never used by
    $\adv$ are simply discarded), and dropping a flag that never influences the
    output changes nothing, so
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g1g2}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm1}^\adv \Rightarrow 1] = \Pr[\printgame{gm2}^\adv \Rightarrow 1].
    \end{equation}

    We have arranged the game so that $\mathbf{Initialize}$ now consists of the
    experiment $\Real_\Sigma(1^\secp, \numOSign)$ of
    \cite[Experiment~2]{C:ABDPW25} (lines $1$, $3$--$5$ and $8$ of
    Figure~\ref{fig:games}) together with the bookkeeping of line $2$, with
    $x$ handed to $\adv$ as the public key and the transcripts $\pi_1 \until
    \pi_\numOSign$ kept as game state. Game $\printgame{gm3}$ replaces
    $\Real_\Sigma(1^\secp, \numOSign)$ by
    $\HintSim_\Sigma(1^\secp, \numOSign, \Sim)$: the witness $w$ is discarded and
    the $i$-th transcript is produced as $\pi_i \gets \Sim(x, \hint_i)$ with
    $\hint_i \gets \hintdistr_x$. Consider the distinguisher $\ddv$ which, on
    input $(x, \pi_1 \until \pi_\numOSign)$, runs $\adv$ on $\pk = x$, answers
    its $\RO$ queries by lazy sampling as in Figure~\ref{fig:games}, answers
    its $\sctr$-th signing query on $\msg$ by parsing $\pi_\sctr$ as
    $(\com_\sctr, \chall_\sctr, \rsp_\sctr)$, programming $\HT[\com_\sctr \concat
    x \concat \msg] \coloneqq \chall_\sctr$ and returning $\pi_\sctr$, and
    finally outputs the bit computed by $\mathbf{Finalize}$. Every step of this
    simulation is efficient and, crucially, uses \emph{only} the statement $x$
    and the public verification algorithm $\Ver_\Sigma$: the witness $w$ is
    needed neither to answer the queries of $\adv$ nor to decide whether $\adv$
    has won. Therefore $\ddv$ is PPT and, writing $\pi = (\pi_1 \until
    \pi_\numOSign)$, we have $\Pr[\ddv(x, \pi) = 1] =
    \Pr[\printgame{gm2}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]$ when $(x, \pi) \gets
    \Real_\Sigma(1^\secp, \numOSign)$, and $\Pr[\ddv(x, \pi) = 1] =
    \Pr[\printgame{gm3}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]$ when $(x, \pi) \gets
    \HintSim_\Sigma(1^\secp, \numOSign, \Sim)$. Hence
    by~\cite[Definition~3.2]{C:ABDPW25}
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g2g3}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm2}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        \leq \Pr[\printgame{gm3}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign}.
    \end{equation}
    This is the step that differs from~\cite{C:ABDPW25}: the swap is performed on
    the \EUFCMA game itself and no guessing has taken place yet, so the loss is a
    single hint-assisted \textsf{wHVZK} advantage with $\numOSign$ hints.

    \subsubsection*{From the simulated game to hint-\textsf{IMP-PA}.}
    From now on the game does not use the witness: it is entirely determined by
    $x$, by $\numOSign$ hints, and by the code of $\Sim$. It remains to turn
    $\adv$ into an impersonator, which is done by the guessing argument
    of~\cite{EC:AABN02}; we recall it in Figure~\Cref{fig:games1,fig:games2}, in the
    order that suits us.

    Game $\printgame{gm4}$ is game $\printgame{gm3}$ with bookkeeping added to
    $\mathbf{Finalize}$: an index $\fpguess \sample \set{1 \until \numRO+1}$ is
    drawn at line $31$ and compared at line $36$ with the index $j$ such that
    $\QT[j] = \com^* \concat x \concat \msg^*$. This index is well defined
    whenever line $34$ is reached. Indeed, by property (ii) the point $u^* =
    \com^* \concat x \concat \msg^*$ was queried to $\RO$ by $\adv$; that query
    was fresh, because the only other way for $\HT[u^*]$ to become defined is a
    signing query, which by injectivity of the encoding would require $\msg^* \in
    \Qset$, excluded by line $32$; hence $u^*$ was appended to $\QT$. Moreover
    $j \leq \hctr \leq \numRO+1$, so $j$ lies in the range from which $\fpguess$
    is drawn.

    The index $\fpguess$ is drawn before both of the early rejections of
    $\mathbf{Finalize}$, hence on every run of the game, and it is used neither
    in the answers to the queries of $\adv$ nor in the computation of the output.
    Consequently the whole run of $\adv$ is independent of $\fpguess$, and hence so
    are both the event $\set{\printgame{gm4}^\adv \Rightarrow 1}$ and the index
    $j$; moreover, on that event $\mathbf{Finalize}$ reaches line $36$, so
    $\goodev_4$ holds if and only if $j = \fpguess$. Hence
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g3g4}
        \begin{aligned}
            \Pr[\printgame{gm3}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
            &= \Pr[\printgame{gm4}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
            = (\numRO+1)\cdot
            \Pr[\printgame{gm4}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge j = \fpguess] \\
            &= (\numRO+1)\cdot
            \Pr[\printgame{gm4}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_4].
        \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}

    Note that \eqref{eq:g3g4} must be phrased through the joint event, and
    \emph{not} in the factorised form $\Pr[\bG \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev] =
    \Pr[\bG \Rightarrow 1]\cdot\Pr[\goodev]$ used in~\cite{EC:AABN02}: because of
    the early rejections at lines $32$ and $35$, which have no counterpart there,
    the flag $\badev$ is also left unset on runs that reject early, so
    $\Pr[\goodev_4] > 1/(\numRO+1)$.

    Game $\printgame{gm5}$ additionally overwrites $\chall^*$ with
    $\HT[\QT[\fpguess]]$ when $j \neq \fpguess$, that is, it runs the verifier on
    the challenge sitting at the \emph{guessed} position rather than at the
    correct one. This modification is line $38$, which follows the setting of
    $\badev$ at line $37$, so $\printgame{gm4}$ and $\printgame{gm5}$ are
    identical-until-$\badev$ and \eqref{eq:good} gives
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g4g5}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm4}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_4]
        = \Pr[\printgame{gm5}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_5].
    \end{equation}

    Game $\printgame{gm6}$ finally draws $\fpguess$ and a challenge $\chall^\circ
    \sample \cC$ in $\mathbf{Initialize}$ and uses $\chall^\circ$ as the answer to
    the $\fpguess$-th fresh $\RO$ query. This is a change of sampling order only.
    In $\printgame{gm5}$ the answers to the fresh $\RO$ queries are independent
    uniform elements of $\cC$ and $\fpguess$ is an independent uniform index; in
    $\printgame{gm6}$ the index is drawn first and, for every fixed value of
    $\fpguess$, the answers to the fresh $\RO$ queries are again independent
    uniform elements of $\cC$. The joint distribution of $\fpguess$ and of the
    view of $\adv$ is therefore the same in both games, and $\mathbf{Finalize}$
    is computed from that data by identical code, so
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g5g6}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm5}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_5]
        = \Pr[\printgame{gm6}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_6].
    \end{equation}

    \subsubsection*{The impersonator.}
    Game $\printgame{gm6}$ is exactly the simulation carried out by the following
    two-stage adversary $\bdv = (\bdv_1, \bdv_2)$ against the
    $\hintdistr$-hint-\textsf{IMP-PA} security of $\Sigma$. On input a statement
    $x$ and hints $\hint_1 \until \hint_\numOSign \gets \hintdistr_x$, the
    algorithm $\bdv_1$ computes $\pi_i \gets \Sim(x, \hint_i)$ for $i = 1 \until
    \numOSign$, draws $\fpguess \sample \set{1 \until \numRO+1}$, and runs $\adv$
    on $\pk = x$, answering its queries as in $\printgame{gm6}$, until $\adv$
    makes its $\fpguess$-th fresh $\RO$ query. It parses that query as $\com^*
    \concat x \concat \msg^*$ and outputs $\com^*$ together with the state of the
    simulation; if $\adv$ halts earlier, $\bdv_1$ outputs an arbitrary
    commitment. On input the challenge $\chall^\circ \sample \cC$, the algorithm
    $\bdv_2$ sets $\HT[\com^* \concat x \concat \msg^*] \coloneqq \chall^\circ$,
    returns $\chall^\circ$ to $\adv$ as the answer to its $\fpguess$-th fresh
    query, resumes the simulation, and outputs the response $\rsp^*$ contained in
    the forgery of $\adv$. Note that $\bdv$ needs neither a transcript oracle nor
    the witness: the $\numOSign$ hints and the simulator $\Sim$ suffice. That
    $\printgame{gm6}$ draws $\chall^\circ$ already in $\mathbf{Initialize}$, whereas
    $\bdv_2$ receives it only after $\bdv_1$ has output $\com^*$, is immaterial:
    the value is first \emph{used} at line $12'$, which is exactly the point at
    which $\bdv_1$ stops.

    We claim that on the event $\set{\printgame{gm6}^\adv \Rightarrow 1} \cap
    \goodev_6$ the conversation of $\bdv$ with the verifier is exactly the
    triple $(\com^*, \chall^\circ, \rsp^*)$, and that it is accepting. On that event $\mathbf{Finalize}$
    reaches line $39$, so neither early rejection fired; since $\goodev_6$ holds
    and line $36$ was evaluated, $j = \fpguess$. Hence $\QT[\fpguess] = u^*$ and,
    by line $35$, $\chall^* = \HT[u^*]$. The
    entry $\HT[u^*]$ was set to $\chall^\circ$ at the $\fpguess$-th fresh $\RO$
    query, and it was not modified afterwards: the only other writer is
    $\OSign$, which programs points of the form $\com \concat x \concat \msg$
    with $\msg \in \Qset$, whereas $\msg^* \notin \Qset$ by line $32$, so
    injectivity of the encoding rules this out. Therefore $\chall^* =
    \chall^\circ$, line $38$ is not executed, and the game returns
    $\Ver_\Sigma(x, \com^*, \chall^\circ, \rsp^*) = 1$, which is precisely the
    winning condition of $\bdv$. Consequently
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:g6b}
        \Pr[\printgame{gm6}^\adv \Rightarrow 1 \wedge \goodev_6]
        \leq \hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}.
    \end{equation}

    Chaining \eqref{eq:g0}--\eqref{eq:g6b} gives
    \begin{align*}
        \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv)
        &\leq \Pr[\printgame{gm3}^\adv \Rightarrow 1]
        + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign}
        + (\numRO+\numOSign+1)\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma} \\
        &\leq (\numRO+1)\cdot\hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}
        + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign} \\
        &\phantom{\leq ~} + (\numRO+\numOSign+1)\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma},
    \end{align*}
    which is the claim. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{The improved \EUFCMA bound}\label{sec:eufcma-gen}

Combining Lemma~\ref{lem:fs-hints} with the special-soundness step
of~\cite[Lemma~3.2]{C:ABDPW25} we obtain the announced generalisation
of~\cite[Theorem~1]{C:ABDPW25}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:EUFCMA-gen}
    Let $(R, \hintdistr)$ be a relation with hints.
    Let $\Sigma$ be a $\Sigma$-protocol for the relation $R$ that has challenge
    space $\cC$ and is special-sound with respect to a soundness relation
    $\tilde{R}$.
    Additionally, let $\Sim$ be a $\hintdistr$-hint-assisted simulator for
    $\Sigma$.
    For any PPT algorithm $\adv$ against the \EUFCMA security of $\SIG[\Sigma]$
    there exist a PPT algorithm $\ddv$ and an expected polynomial time algorithm
    $\cE$ such that
    \begin{align*}
        \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv)
        &\leq \left(\numRO+1\right)\cdot
        \left(\hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE}{\numOSign}
        + \frac{1}{\lvert \cC \rvert}\right) \\
        &\phantom{\leq ~}
        + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign}
        + (\numRO + \numOSign + 1)\,\numOSign \cdot \minentropy{\Sigma},
    \end{align*}
    where $\numRO$ and $\numOSign$ are upper bounds on the number of queries that
    $\adv$ makes to $\RO$ and $\OSign$, respectively.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Let $\bdv$ and $\ddv$ be the algorithms given by Lemma~\ref{lem:fs-hints}, so
    that
    \[
        \begin{aligned}
            \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv)
            &\leq (\numRO+1)\cdot\hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}
            + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign} \\
            &\phantom{\leq ~}
            + (\numRO+\numOSign+1)\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma}.
        \end{aligned}
    \]
    Since $\Sigma$ is special sound with respect to $\tilde{R}$,
    \cite[Lemma~3.2]{C:ABDPW25} applied to the two-stage adversary $\bdv$
    produces an expected polynomial time algorithm $\cE$ for the relation with
    hints $(\tilde{R}, \hintdistr)$ such that
    \[
        \hintimppaadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\bdv}{\numOSign}
        \leq \hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE}{\numOSign}
        + \frac{1}{\abs{\cC}}.
    \]
    Substituting gives the claim. If a strict PPT algorithm is required, one
    applies~\cite[Corollary~3.1]{C:ABDPW25} to $\cE$: this yields a PPT algorithm
    $\cE'$ with $\hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE}{\numOSign} \leq 2 \cdot
    \hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE'}{\numOSign}$, so that the first term
    of the bound becomes $(\numRO+1)\bigl(2 \cdot
    \hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE'}{\numOSign} + 1/\abs{\cC}\bigr)$.
    Note that $\cE'$ runs in polynomial time in $\secp$ only when the success
    probability of $\cE$ is the inverse of a polynomial. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Comparison with~{\cite[Theorem~1]{C:ABDPW25}}]\label{rem:comparison}
    The bound of~\cite[Theorem~1]{C:ABDPW25} reads
    \[
        \begin{aligned}
            \Advantage{\EUFCMA}{\SIG[\Sigma]}(\adv)
            &\leq (\numRO+1)\cdot
            \left(\hintreladv{\tilde{R}}{\hintdistr}{\cE}{\numOSign}
            + \hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\bdv, \numOSign}
            + \frac{1}{\abs{\cC}}\right) \\
            &\phantom{\leq ~}
            + (\numRO+\numOSign+1)\numOSign\cdot\minentropy{\Sigma}.
        \end{aligned}
    \]
    Theorem~\ref{thm:EUFCMA-gen} improves on it by taking the hint-assisted
    \textsf{wHVZK} term out of the factor $\numRO+1$; all the other terms, the
    number of hints and the running times of the constructed algorithms are
    unchanged, so the improvement comes for free. It matters whenever the
    hint-assisted simulator is only \emph{statistically} close to the honest
    prover with a gap that is not overwhelmingly small. This is exactly the
    situation for the odd-degree variant of \sqisign{}: the hint distribution has
    a failure branch, taken with probability at most $1/\epsilon$, on which no
    odd-degree response isogeny of degree below $\responseupperbound$ exists, and
    this branch contributes a term $\numOSign/\epsilon$ to
    $\hinthvzkadv{\Sigma}{\hintdistr}{\Sim}{\ddv, \numOSign}$ on top of the terms
    that are already present in the even-degree case. In the bound
    of~\cite[Theorem~1]{C:ABDPW25} that contribution would instead be
    $(\numRO+1)\,\numOSign/\epsilon$. For the same security claim, $\log\epsilon$
    may therefore be smaller by $\log \numRO$ bits, which with the NIST bound
    $\numRO \leq 2^{64}$ is what makes the smaller values of $\log \epsilon$ in
    Table~\ref{tab:timing} admissible.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why this order of the game hops]\label{rem:order}
    The reordering performed in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:fs-hints} is
    constrained from both sides, and it is not free.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item The replacement of the honest transcripts by simulated ones must
            come \emph{after} the hop $\printgame{gm0} \to \printgame{gm1}$. In
            $\printgame{gm0}$ the challenge of a signature is the value of the
            random oracle at $\com \concat x \concat \msg$, which need not be
            uniform and independent of the rest, since $\adv$ may have queried
            that point before. The experiments $\Real_\Sigma$ and
            $\HintSim_\Sigma$ of~\cite[Experiment~2]{C:ABDPW25} produce
            transcripts with \emph{independent uniform} challenges, so they can
            only be plugged in once the signing oracle has been made to sample
            the challenge itself and to reprogram the random oracle accordingly.
            This is what the min-entropy term pays for.
        \item The replacement must come \emph{before} the guessing hops
            $\printgame{gm3} \to \printgame{gm6}$, since those are what introduce
            the factor $\numRO+1$.
    \end{enumerate}
    In particular, Lemma~\ref{lem:fs-hints} cannot be obtained by composing
    \cite[Lemma~2.2]{C:ABDPW25} with \cite[Lemma~3.1]{C:ABDPW25} as black boxes:
    the game hop has to be inserted \emph{inside} the proof
    of~\cite[Lemma~3.5]{EC:AABN02}, between the games that isolate the
    reprogramming failure and the games that perform the guess. As a by-product,
    the min-entropy of the \emph{simulated} commitment distribution is never
    needed: from $\printgame{gm1}$ on the signing oracle reprograms the random
    oracle unconditionally, so only $\minentropy{\Sigma}$, the min-entropy of the
    honest protocol, appears in the bound.
\end{remark}
